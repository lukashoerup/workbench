# The pitch was reviewed in character as Lasse, and it failed

20 Aug 2026. A separate model was given his role — TV producer, not technical,
twenty minutes before a budget meeting — and asked to read the pitch page and
be harsh. It was, and it was right. The page has been rebuilt to its
prescription.

## Where he stopped reading

At the section showing a full prompt in English. *"There is half a page of
machine text… and at that moment the page stopped talking to me and started
talking to itself. I don't commission television by reading the camera manual
aloud to the cameraman either."*

He had already half-left earlier, at the retelling of the case: **four dense
paragraphs explaining to him the case that he sent us.**

## What he called irrelevant, and what got cut

The English prompt block. The consistency-enforcement section. The
first-pass workshop diary — the car, the burned-in timecode, the handprints —
*"that is your workshop journal. I don't want to see how the sausage is made,
I want to taste it."* The transcription status and the free tier's 30-minute
limit. The motion-tier table with percentages, which he reduced to one
sentence himself: most of it is stills with a calm camera move, and those never
fail. And the repository path in the footer, *"an address inside your machine"*.

All of it is now gone from the pitch. It lives here, in the docs, where it
belongs.

## What he kept

The camera lines under each frame — *"85 mm, f/1.8, camera at table height"*.
*"That is film language, not computer language. Keep those."* Worth noting: the
one piece of apparent jargon a producer wants **more** of is the technical
language of his own craft.

The rails section, unchanged: *"that is the page I show a channel lawyer."*
And the honest admission that a real reconstruction is still better: *"that
honesty makes me more comfortable than all your tests."*

## The three real gaps

Not style problems. Things a commissioning editor asks in minute three and the
page could not answer:

1. **Who is journalistically accountable?** The page knows Ekstra Bladet
   published on 30 September 1999, which the podcast does not say with a date —
   so someone researched the real case. *"Who? Who guarantees that no picture
   contradicts the court record? Is there a journalist in the chain or only a
   machine?"* There is not. There needs to be, and it is now stated as a role
   that does not yet exist and cannot be skipped.
2. **Rights.** *"Do we even have the right to build television on someone
   else's podcast?"* No. That is a first phone call to Bauer Media, not a last
   one.
3. **The bereaved.** The victim had three daughters who are alive today.
   *"The rules about faces are fine, but a channel asks about the family, not
   about pixels."*

## What he could not follow

**The chain.** Not because it was absent but because it was scattered across
three sections. He asked for it in one place, in five sentences, and he was
right that it was never written that way. It now is, with the two gates he
personally sits on marked in the margin.

He also could not tell **whom he would say "colder" to, or how long it would
take to see the result.** A production plan needs both. Both are now answered.

## The contradiction he caught

The page said "8 pictures made · 58 kr" at the top and "54 pictures and two
clips cost 58 kroner" further down. *"A contradiction like that on the front
page of a budget, and a channel closes the folder."* Fixed.

## What he demanded, and now has

Pictures first, full width, one per timecode, no introduction. *"The pictures
ARE the argument — if they hold, I read on; if not, the rest is irrelevant."*

And above all: **show me the clip.** He was given two stills and the caption
"6 seconds later". *"The entire question is whether it moves credibly, and
precisely there I get a paragraph of prose."* Four clips are now embedded and
play in place.

Finally, the two frames he liked best — the newspapers printed side down, and
the washed floor with the clean rectangle — were buried at the bottom under
"EXTRA". They are now in the main sequence.

## The verdict

> *"Yes, Bull House will spend money on the next step — because the idea of
> never showing a face solves quality, control and scandal risk in one move,
> and the price is a tenth of a shooting day — but the next krone goes to a
> television set and one finished ninety-second clip with narration, not to
> more pages like this one."*

That is now the only next step the page proposes.

## The lesson worth keeping

The material was not too technical because of the words in it. It was too
technical because it was **organised around how the thing was built rather
than around what the reader has to decide.** Every cut section was true,
useful, and correctly placed — in the docs. None of it belonged in the pitch.
